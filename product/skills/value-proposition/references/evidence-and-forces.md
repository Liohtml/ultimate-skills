# Evidence and Switching Forces

Read this when turning raw research (interviews, tickets, reviews, sales notes, usage data) into labelled evidence for a value proposition, or when mapping the four switching forces.

## Contents
- Evidence sources ranked by strength
- Extracting evidence: tagging and the quote bank
- Pattern vs anecdote

- Switch-interview prompts

## Evidence sources ranked by strength

| Strength | Source | Why |
|----------|--------|-----|
| Strongest | Observed behaviour and usage data; money or commitment given | People did it, not said it |
| Strong | Interviews about specific past events ("the last time you...") | Reconstructs real decisions |
| Medium | Support tickets, reviews, sales and churn notes, win/loss records | Real, unprompted, but filtered by who complains or who talks to sales |
| Weak | Surveys of stated preference, "would you use" questions | Stated intent overestimates behaviour |
| Weakest | Internal opinion, competitor feature lists, analyst trend pieces | Says nothing about this segment |

Use weaker sources to find hypotheses and stronger ones to confirm them.

## Extracting evidence

1. Read each source end to end before tagging; context changes meaning.
2. Copy snippets **verbatim**. Paraphrase loses the vocabulary you will need for messaging.
3. Tag each snippet with: segment, job, force (push / pull / anxiety / habit), pain or gain, current workaround, trigger, desired outcome.
4. Give every snippet a source ID (interview number, ticket ID, review URL, query name) and a date.
5. Convert feature requests into the problem behind them: "I need a Slack integration" → "Ask: what happens today when an invoice becomes overdue, and who needs to know?"

### Quote bank format

| ID | Source | Segment | Verbatim | Tags | Strength |
|----|--------|---------|----------|------|----------|
| Q1 | Interview 4, 2026-03-12 | Agency owner, 12 people | "I lose every Friday afternoon chasing invoices." | Push, pain: time, workaround: manual email | Strong |
| Q2 | Ticket 8812 | Ops lead | "Is there a way to stop the reminder when the client already paid by bank transfer?" | Anxiety, pain: embarrassment | Medium |

The quote bank feeds three later steps: Why / What before (evidence), forces (ratings), and the messaging phrase bank (customer vocabulary).

## Pattern vs anecdote

- Count **independent sources**, not mentions. One talkative customer saying it five times is one source.
- Rough rule within one segment: 1 source = anecdote; 2 = signal; 3 or more independent sources = pattern worth putting in the value proposition.
- Note contradicting evidence explicitly. A value proposition that hides counter-evidence will fail in sales calls instead.

## Traps when reading research

| Trap | Example | What to do |
|------|---------|------------|
| Future tense | "I would definitely use that" | Discount; ask what they did last time |
| Compliments | "Love the idea!" | Ignore as evidence of value |
| Leading questions in the source | "Wouldn't it be great if...?" | Discard the answer |
| Generalisations | "We always struggle with cash flow" | Look for the specific instance; if none, mark as weak |
| Survivorship | Only interviewing happy customers | Add churned users and non-customers who chose an alternative |
| Mixing segments | Freelancer and agency quotes in one list | Split by segment before counting |

## Switch-interview prompts

A switch interview reconstructs one real decision as a timeline. Interview people who recently switched (to you or to a competitor) or recently considered switching and did not. For the full interview structure use the interview-script skill; these prompts focus on the forces.

| Stage | Prompts | Force revealed |
|-------|---------|----------------|
| First thought | "When did you first think the old way wasn't working? What was happening that day?" | Push |
| Passive looking | "What did you notice or try before you started really looking?" | Push, habit |
| Trigger event | "What happened that made you start looking seriously, right then?" | Push (the trigger) |
| Active looking | "What did you look at? What did you hope it would let you do?" | Pull |
| Deciding | "What almost stopped you? What did you need to be sure about?" | Anxiety |
| Holding on | "What did you have to give up or change? What did you keep doing the old way?" | Habit |
| After | "What is different now? What did you expect that didn't happen?" | Pull confirmed or not |

### Rating rubric

| Rating | Meaning |
|--------|---------|
| 1 | Rarely mentioned, mild |
| 3 | Recurring across several sources, moderate intensity |
| 5 | In most sources, emotionally charged, or it triggered action (they started looking, or they backed out) |

### Reading the net

- **Push + Pull clearly above Anxiety + Habit:** the value proposition can focus on the outcome; keep proof points for the remaining anxiety.
- **Roughly equal:** the deal is won or lost on anxiety and habit. Put proof, guarantees, migration help and compatibility into the offer and the top of the messaging.
- **Anxiety + Habit higher:** more features will not convert this segment. Either reduce switching cost substantially or target a segment with a stronger push (a recent trigger event, an outgrown workaround).
