# Messaging Hierarchy Handoff

Read this when turning a finished value proposition into a messaging hierarchy that marketing, sales and onboarding can work from. This is the brief, not the final copy; copy written from it goes to copy-editing (writing plugin) for review.

## Contents
- The hierarchy template
- Rules for each level
- Mapping forces to messages
- Channel adaptations
- Handoff checklist

## The hierarchy template

```
Segment: [one best-fit segment]          Category (from positioning): [category]
Primary alternative: [what they use today]

1. Value statement (internal, 1-2 sentences)
   [Outcome] for [segment] who [push], unlike [alternative], because [key difference].

2. Headline (external)
   [Primary outcome, in customer words]

3. Subhead
   [For whom + how, one sentence]

4. Value pillars (3, at most 4)
   Pillar A: [benefit]  | Proof: [number, quote, demo] | Enabled by: [features]
   Pillar B: [benefit]  | Proof: [...]                 | Enabled by: [...]
   Pillar C: [benefit]  | Proof: [...]                 | Enabled by: [...]

5. Objection handling (from Anxiety and Habit)
   "[Objection in customer words]" -> [answer + proof or offer element]

6. Proof inventory
   Quotes (IDs), metrics (source, n), logos/case studies, guarantees, certifications

7. Voice-of-customer phrase bank
   Phrases to use: [verbatim from quote bank]
   Phrases to avoid: [internal jargon, unproven claims]

8. Claims not yet allowed
   [Claims that depend on untested hypotheses, with the hypothesis ID]
```

## Rules for each level

- **One primary message.** The headline carries the strongest pull for the segment's top-ranked pain or gain. If two candidates compete, message-test them rather than combining them into a vague sentence.
- **Pillars are benefits, not feature groups.** "Stop chasing payments" is a pillar; "Automation" is a feature category.
- **Every pillar has proof.** A pillar without proof is a hypothesis; mark it and keep it out of external copy until tested, or soften the claim.
- **Features appear last** and only as the reason the benefit is believable.
- **Use the customer's vocabulary.** If customers say "chasing", do not write "accounts-receivable workflow optimisation".
- **Claims follow evidence.** Anything in "claims not yet allowed" stays out of external copy. This list protects sales from promising what the product cannot yet prove.

## Mapping forces to messages

| Force | Where it shows up | Example |
|-------|-------------------|---------|
| Push | Headline or hero problem statement; ads to passive seekers | "Still losing Friday afternoons to payment reminders?" |
| Pull | Headline, pillars | "Get paid on time without being the bad cop." |
| Anxiety | Objection handling, proof near the call to action, guarantees, trial terms | "Preview every reminder before it goes out. Stops automatically when the client pays." |
| Habit | Setup and migration messaging, integration logos, "works with what you have" | "Connects to the invoicing tool you already use. Set up in ten minutes." |

## Channel adaptations

| Channel | Leads with | Uses from the hierarchy | Notes |
|---------|-----------|-------------------------|-------|
| Homepage or landing page | Headline, subhead | Pillars, proof, objections near the call to action | One segment per page when segments differ |
| Paid ads | Push or pull, one idea per ad | Headline variants, phrase bank | Test framings against each other; measure qualified clicks |
| Sales deck and talk track | Push (the cost of the status quo) | Alternatives, forces, proof, objections | Old way → new way → proof; keep honest comparisons |
| Onboarding (first run, emails) | The first outcome they can reach | Pillar order becomes the activation sequence | Deliver the first pillar's value fast; address habit with import and defaults |
| Pricing page | Value per plan | Pillars mapped to plans, objection handling | Anxiety peaks here: guarantees, cancellation terms, FAQ |
| In-product empty states | The job the screen serves | Phrase bank | One sentence; the pull, not the feature |

## Handoff checklist

Give the copywriter, marketer or sales lead:
- [ ] The hierarchy (sections 1-8 above), with the segment named
- [ ] The quote bank with source IDs
- [ ] Proof assets and their sources
- [ ] The list of claims not yet allowed, and when each might be cleared (hypothesis and test date)
- [ ] Positioning inputs: category, primary alternative, best-fit customer
- [ ] Tone and voice guidance if it exists

Then:
- Copy for pages, emails or ads goes to copy-editing (writing plugin) for review.
- Persona-specific variants draw on user-personas; keep one hierarchy per segment rather than one hierarchy stretched across segments.
- When a hypothesis passes, move its claim from "not yet allowed" into proof; when it fails, remove the claim everywhere it was drafted.
